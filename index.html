How ROADWATCH Works

A transparent, end-to-end workflow bridging citizens and highway engineering crews.

1

Report Damage

Citizens upload photographs of damaged road surfaces with automatic browser GPS location detection.

2

Review & Triage

Municipal engineers review severity (High, Medium, Low), approve legitimate reports, and generate maintenance tasks.

3

Dispatch Crew

Repair tasks are prioritized, deadlines are set, and qualified asphalt and drainage workers are assigned to the field.

4

Verify & Complete

Field workers upload after-repair photographs. The chief engineer inspects side-by-side comparison before marking resolved.

Advanced Platform Capabilities

GIS Interactive Mapping

Leaflet-powered spatial map with color-coded severity markers (Red, Orange, Green), radius search, and live GPS positioning.

Before & After Verification

High-definition side-by-side photographic quality assurance. No repair is marked complete without engineering sign-off.

IoT Sensor Telemetry

Demonstration module showing continuous vibration amplitude and International Roughness Index (IRI) monitoring.

Drone Aerial Survey

Scheduled aerial corridor missions, photogrammetry inspection reports, and direct linkage to citizen damage complaints.

Classified Hazards

Road Damage Categories

Comprehensive classification system to dispatch appropriate equipment and materials.

Potholes

Cavities formed by moisture penetration and traffic stress. Requires cold/hot asphalt compaction.

Road Cracks & Fissures

Longitudinal and alligator cracks treated with hot-pour bituminous sealant to prevent structural sub-base collapse.

Storm Waterlogging

Inundation caused by blocked storm drain culverts. Managed by municipal drainage pumps and silt desilting.

Broken Road Surfaces

Eroded wearing courses revealing aggregate stones, causing extreme tire wear and vehicle deceleration.

Uneven & Rutted Roads

Corrugations and ruts created by heavy freight vehicles, requiring road milling and asphalt re-profiling.

Damaged Footpaths & Curbs

Pedestrian safety hazards including dislodged interlocking pavers, broken curbs, and exposed utility trenches.
